02 / What users should expect

Principles only matter when the product keeps them.

Impala Streamer is designed to remain loyal to the people using it. That promise shapes what the product does, what it refuses to do, and how it behaves when conditions are imperfect.

01

The user is the principal

Impala Streamer exists to serve the person using it—not an advertising model, an engagement target, or a recurring billing strategy. Product decisions begin with user purpose and respect the time, attention, media, and relationships entrusted to the system.

Why it matters: A product cannot claim to empower users while quietly optimizing against their interests.

02

Responsibility stays with the provider

Discrete Development remains accountable for the systems it designs and the providers it chooses. Workflows, automation, and vendors can carry out tasks, but they cannot carry responsibility or become excuses when something affects the user.

Why it matters: Trust begins when accountability has a clear home.

03

Privacy is a visible choice

Private libraries need more than a login screen. Authentication, invited access, ownership boundaries, and optional external services are made understandable. When a capability introduces a privacy tradeoff, the user receives a meaningful way to control it.

Why it matters: Consent is meaningful only when people can understand a choice and say no without losing the product's core value.

04

The system absorbs the friction

Complexity should be handled by the system wherever practical, not passed to users as setup rituals, ambiguous errors, or daily maintenance. When intervention is necessary, Impala provides useful information and clear next steps.

Why it matters: Ownership should create control, not a second job.

05

Dependability before expansion

Loading, pausing, resuming, switching sources, recovering from interruption, and explaining what happened are part of the product—not background details. Modular services allow new capabilities to grow from stable boundaries without fragmenting the experience people already trust.

Why it matters: Growth has value only when it preserves the reliability and purpose that earned the user's trust.

The operating model

Promises are tested in real conditions.

Personal media lives across mixed hardware, changing networks, interrupted sessions, different levels of technical fluency, and libraries that grow over time. The product must respect its principles under those conditions—not only in a demonstration.

The product culture values testable behavior, small reversible changes, useful diagnostics, explicit boundaries, and feedback from people who use the system in everyday life.

“It just works” is not a marketing phrase here. It is an obligation: the system recovers, communicates, and respects the user's intent when conditions are less than perfect.
