01 / The problem worth solving

Systems should empower people — not shield providers from responsibility.

Impala Streamer is built on the Impala Architecture — a philosophy of design that puts the user first, purpose at the center, and revenue where it belongs: as a consequence of doing good work.

The modern software model lost the plot.

Personal software used to be built around what people needed. Today, it's built around what businesses can extract. Revenue strategy replaced user purpose, and people with the least time, money, or technical flexibility often pay the highest cost.

That shift turned users into inventory.

The user becomes a data source. Every session becomes a monetization opportunity. Attention is treated as fuel. Control is treated as optional. The person using the system is no longer the customer — they're the product.

The gap isn't a missing feature. It's a missing philosophy.

People aren't asking for less capability. They're asking for systems that behave responsibly. Clear playback states. Sensible boundaries. Reliable recovery. Interfaces that respect time instead of consuming it. These are not luxuries — they're the baseline of good work.

In personal media, the problem appears as subscriptions layered onto libraries people already own, promotional screens placed between a person and their collection, behavioral tracking, unclear privacy boundaries, and technical friction pushed back onto the user. Impala Streamer exists to replace those compromises with direct access, understandable choices, and dependable playback.

The responsibility lies with the provider

People should not need to decode clever pricing structures, interpret systems designed to justify subscription fees, or investigate how their daily habits are being harvested to fuel marketing campaigns. The burden of clarity, honesty, and responsible design belongs to the people who build the software — not the people who use it.

Impala has been built on these principles from day one.

Impala and Impala Streamer are designed with those commitments at the center. Every feature is evaluated against the philosophy it came from. If a new capability introduces the possibility of violating that covenant, we add controls to disable it and explain the risks clearly — giving the user the ability to choose their own posture.
