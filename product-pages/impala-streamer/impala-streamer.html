Impala Streamer Architecture

The design and architecture of a system can either stress the system unnecessarily, inviting failure, or it can help distribute that load across the entire structure, enhancing performance and reliability. The component that every service has in common is that they need a player to connect to the world. That player is built into the core, and the services are built as independent collaborative modules. Their tasks never impact the core service — even when your library grows to thousands of movies and tens of thousands of songs. Impala Streamer is designed to remain fast and responsive at its core.

A Response to a Frustrating Industry Trend

I have been curating my personal media library for nearly 20 years, 13 of which have included personal media streamers. When my collection reached a moderate size, features like metadata, album art, and movie posters began causing unacceptable lag. It was simply too much work for one machine — managing all that content for thousands of movies and tens of thousands of songs — when all I want to do is start a movie or find that song I haven't been able to get out of my head all day.

Impala Streamer solves this by separating those tasks into independent services that never interfere with the main purpose of its existence: to play the media you want played, when you want it played — not after it has finished processing other tasks. Impala Streamer respects your time and removes that friction. The way it is supposed to be.
